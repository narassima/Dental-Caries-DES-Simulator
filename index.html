Mathematical Formulation & Simulation Mechanics

Implements the discrete event simulation logic described in the research manuscript.

1. Camp Queuing Logic (Lindley Recursion)

Each school camp operates for an 8-hour shift (480 minutes). Students arrive from classrooms as a stochastic morning batch of size N ~ Ceil(U(8,12)) × (1 + TS%). The triage auxiliary admits each student in FIFO order:

A_done[j] = ∑ adm[k],   with adm ~ Uniform(0.5, 1.5) minutes

For any clinical server r ∈ {dentist, auxiliary, teacher}, student j begins service at:

Start[j] = max(Ready[j], Free_Time[r]),   Served if Start[j] < 480 min

Operational Rule: If Start[j] ≥ 480 min, the procedure cannot commence and the child is classified as cut off due to shift conclusion.

2. 10-Month School-Year Layer & Pool Depletion

In months t = 2...10, repeat maintenance visits consume a share ρ (standard 15%):

Rep[g] = min(ρ × S[g], U[g]),   New[g] = min(S[g] - Rep[g], P[g] - U[g])

Where S[g] is camp slot capacity in grade cohort g, U[g] is cumulative unique students treated so far, and P[g] is the total caries prevalent pool. Repeat visits are subtracted to ensure only unique individuals count toward population coverage.

3. Health Outcomes: Years Lived with Disability (YLDs)

Calculates disability averted using Global Burden of Disease (GBD 2019) disability weights with continuous 3% annual social discounting:

YLD_dur = ∑ Eff_New[g, i] × DW[i] × [ (1 - e^(-0.03 × dur[i])) / 0.03 ]

Where dur[i] represents the clinical longevity of the intervention (3 years for ART restorations, 2 years for fluoride varnish).

4. Dedicated School Dental Workforce Gap

Dedicated_Dentists_Needed = Unmet_Need / 1,889

Denominator 1,889 represents the empirical annual reach of 1 dedicated school dentist conducting 16 camps/month for 10 months with team delegation (11.8 new children per camp).

Platform Overview & Quick Start (In 3 Minutes)

Core Concepts

What is the Dental Caries Discrete Event Simulation (DES) Platform?

This application simulates school-based oral health outreach programs using Discrete Event Simulation (DES) based on the Lindley recursion. In public health research and policy design, naive ratio calculations (such as dividing total children by total dentists) frequently produce dangerously over-optimistic forecasts because they ignore the operational realities of delivering care in school settings.

This platform solves that problem by explicitly incorporating four real-world operational mechanisms:

  • Patient-Level Stochastic Queuing: Children do not arrive in a steady, frictionless stream. They arrive in morning batches from classrooms, queue outside screening stations, and wait for clinical bays to become available.
  • Strict 480-Minute (8-Hour) Shift Limit: School dental camps operate strictly between 09:00 AM and 05:00 PM. Any child whose treatment cannot commence before minute 480 must be cut off and sent back to class unserved.
  • Multidisciplinary Task Delegation: Compares solo dentist practice (where the dentist performs every step—screening, fluoride varnish, restorations, and health education—alone) against task delegation (where dental auxiliaries handle fluoride varnish and classroom teachers deliver oral health education), freeing the dentist for operative treatment.
  • 10-Month Academic Pool Depletion & Recall Dilution: In months 2–10 of a school year, high-risk children require maintenance/recall visits for repeat fluoride varnish. This simulation subtracts recall visits so that only unique children treated are counted toward population coverage.
Quick Start in 3 Easy Steps:
  1. Select Your Country Scope (Top Header): Default is India (empirical 35 States & UTs with observed UDISE+ and National Health Profile data). Or switch to Custom Country to enter your own national/provincial parameters.
  2. Explore Feasibility Tiers (Tab 1: National Overview): Click any of the 4 tier cards (Status Quo, Plausible, Stretch, Theoretical Max) or move the 5 operational sliders to see instant shifts in national coverage, unserved children, and dentist workforce deficits.
  3. Watch the Clinic in Action (Tab 2: School Clinic Simulation): Switch to Tab 2 and click Start Animation to watch children move through triage, fluoride varnish bays, and dental chairs in real time, or click Instant Run for full 8-hour shift metrics.

Tab 1: National Overview & Policy Forecasting

Macro Policy Horizon

1. Purpose & Strategic Scope

The National Overview tab aggregates discrete event simulation outcomes over a full 10-month academic school year across all government school children (Classes 1–10). It models how macroscopic policy choices (such as camp frequency, dentist deployment, and team task-shifting) translate into nationwide cavity treatment, population coverage, and public health workforce requirements.

2. What Users Can Do (Interactive Controls)

  • Select Feasibility Tiers (Table 1 Benchmarks): Click any of the 4 policy tier cards to load the predefined operational benchmarks:
    • Status Quo (Current Practice): 2 camps/month per public dentist, 0% task-shifting (solo dentist), 50% participation (φ = 0.5). Represents current baseline delivery in India.
    • Plausible (Recommended Policy): 8 camps/month (2 school days/week), task delegation with 30% more referrals (auxiliaries deliver fluoride varnish, teachers deliver oral health education), 50% participation (φ = 0.5).
    • Stretch (Expanded Outreach): 12 camps/month (3 school days/week), 30% task delegation, 80% public dentist participation (φ = 0.8).
    • Theoretical Maximum: 22 camps/month (daily visits every working day), 30% task delegation, 100% public dentist participation (φ = 1.0). Demonstrates upper physical bound of the current public dental cadre.
  • Adjust 5 Synchronized What-If Policy Sliders: Customize any parameter to test non-standard scenarios:
    • Monthly Camps / Dentist (c): Sliders range from 2 to 22 camps/month. Moving this slider directly alters provider camp time and child reach.
    • Task-Shifting / Team Delegation (TS %): 0% means no delegation. At 10%, 20% or 30%, OHE goes to the teacher and fluoride varnish to the auxiliary, with that many more referrals per camp.
    • Public Dentists Participating (φ): Adjust from 10% to 100% of the state's sanctioned dental workforce mobilized for school visits.
    • Follow-Up / Maintenance Visits (ρ): Adjust from 5% to 50% to set the proportion of camp slots in months 2–10 allocated to repeat recall visits for high-risk children.
    • OHE Need Reduction (R): Adjust from 0% to 5% to model a percentage-point reduction in the share of students needing OHE.
  • Inspect Metric Explanations: Click the small blue i button on any KPI card to open a detailed modal explaining the clinical definition, mathematical formula, and empirical data sources.
  • Analyze Tab 1 Visualizations: Directly view Figure S1 (Coverage Across Feasibility Tiers) and Figure S2 (Age-Specific Reach Distribution across Primary, Upper Primary, and Secondary grades).

Underlying Logic: Uses multi-month pool depletion. In Month 1, 100% of camp slots treat new children. In Months 2–10, recall visits (ρ × S_g) are subtracted, so children are credited only once toward population reach.
Clinical & Policy Significance: Reflects true individual children rescued from untreated decay.

Critical Policy Insight: Under Status Quo, national reach is a dismal 2.37%. The recommended Plausible policy achieves 12.03% (a 5.1-fold expansion). Crucially, even if 100% of public dentists conducted daily camps (Theoretical Max), coverage caps at 51.15%, proving that universal school coverage cannot be achieved by public dentists alone without dedicated school dental cadres.

Children Still Waiting (Unmet Need)
What it measures: Caries-affected government school students who remain completely unserved at year's end: Total Caries Burden - Children Treated.
Clinical Significance: Untreated dental caries in children does not resolve spontaneously; it progresses to pulp necrosis, periapical abscess, facial cellulitis, severe toothache, systemic infection, impaired mastication, undernutrition, and school absenteeism.
Status Quo: 38.53M | Plausible: 34.72M | Stretch: 28.67M | Max: 19.28M

What it measures: Number of full-time dedicated school dental surgeons needed to eliminate the unmet cavity burden in a single school year.
Formula: Children Still Waiting / 1,889. (1 dedicated school dentist conducting 16 camps/month for 10 months with team delegation treats ~1,889 unique children/year).
Policy Utility: Directly informs Ministry of Health and state treasury human resource budgetary requests.
Status Quo: 20,396 | Plausible: 18,379 | Stretch: 15,176 | Max: 10,206
Dentist Utilisation (%)
What it measures: Procedure minutes delivered at camps as a percentage of all working minutes of available dentists.
Formula: Procedure minutes / (Available dentists × 22 days × 480 min × 10 months).
Interpretation: At 8 camps/month, procedures take 14.5% of working time. Camp days themselves take 8 of 22 working days (36%), so this figure is not the share of time left for clinics.
Status Quo: 4.0% | Plausible: 14.5% | Stretch: 21.7% | Max: 39.8%
Disability Averted (YLDs)
What it measures: Years Lived with Disability (YLDs) averted among treated children, calculated using Global Burden of Disease (GBD) disability weights.
In-Year YLDs: 7,110 YLDs averted within the remainder of the calendar school year.
Duration-Adjusted YLDs: 22,222 YLDs capturing the duration of effect of each intervention (2 years for ART and pulpectomy, 3 years for crowns and GIC/composite) discounted at 3% per year.
Plausible: 7,110 In-Year | 22,222 Duration-Adjusted YLDs

Tab 2: School Dental Clinic Simulation & 2D Floorplan Visualizer

Ground-Level Queuing Engine

1. Purpose & Micro-Level Simulation Mechanics

While Tab 1 analyzes national 10-month outcomes, Tab 2 models the ground-level operational reality of an 8-hour (480-minute) school dental camp running from 09:00 AM to 05:00 PM. It simulates individual student arrivals, waiting line build-up, triage decisions, workstation procedure times, and the 480-minute shift cut-off.

2. What Users Can Do (Interactive Controls)

  • Adjust Camp Operational Sliders: Located directly above the playback controls, you can adjust parameters without switching tabs:
    • Task-Shifting (TS%): 0% (Solo Dentist) vs 30% (Team Delegation with Auxiliary & Teacher).
    • Monthly Camps / Dentist: 2 to 22 camps.
    • Cohort Batch Size Scale: Scale student arrival volume from 0.5x to 2.0x.
    • OHE need reduction (R): 0 to 5 percentage points.
    • Dentist Participation (φ): Share of dentists participating.
  • Playback Control Bar:
    • Start Animation / Pause: Starts or freezes the camp clock, advancing time in steps of 2 simulation minutes.
    • Step (+2 min): Manually advance simulation time by one step to inspect individual transitions.
    • Reset: Clears the facility and samples a fresh stochastic batch of students with realistic ages, cavity severities, and arrival times.
    • Instant Run: Instantly executes the entire 480-minute camp day in milliseconds, displaying final metrics and Gantt charts.
    • Speed Chips: Toggle simulation speed between 0.5x (slow-motion observation), 1x (normal), 2x (fast), and 5x (turbo).
  • Dynamic Bottleneck Alert: Real-time banner evaluating clinical flow:
    • Amber Bottleneck: Active when Task-Shifting = 0% (Solo Dentist). The dentist must perform triage, fluoride varnish, and fillings alone. A queue of 8+ children forms outside Chair 3A while other stations remain vacant.
    • Green Optimal Flow: Active when Task-Shifting is above 0%. Dental auxiliaries handle fluoride varnish and teachers deliver oral health education, leaving Chair 3A for clinical procedures.
  • Interactive 2D Clinic Floorplan:
    • Waiting Hall / Reception: Displays arriving students. Click ANY student chip (e.g. #4 (Age 9-11)) to open their Detailed Patient Clinical Chart Modal displaying individual tooth status, assigned provider, procedure start/end times, and wait duration.
    • Bay 2: Auxiliary Triage Desk: Dental auxiliary admits each student in FIFO order (Uniform 0.5 to 1.5 minutes) and routes them based on diagnosed condition.
    • Bay 3A: Dentist Operatory Chair: Dental surgeon performing restorative care (ART, GIC, pulpectomy, extractions). Features a live progress bar indicating % completion of the active procedure.
    • Bay 3B: Preventive Fluoride Bay: Dental auxiliary applying fluoride varnish.
    • Bay 3C: Teacher Oral Health Room: Classroom teacher delivering oral health education.
    • Bay 4: Discharge Lounge: Displays successfully treated children as they finish care.
  • 4-Track Multi-Provider Gantt Scheduling Timeline: Displays 4 horizontal tracks: Dentist Chair, Auxiliary Triage, Auxiliary Fluoride, and Teacher Room. Each procedure block is color-coded by treatment type (Screening, Fluoride Varnish, ART Restoration, Pulpectomy, Extraction). Hover over any block to inspect exact minute timestamps.
  • Live Simulation Event Log: Scrollable live terminal feed logging student admissions, workstation transfers, procedure completions, and 480-minute cut-offs.

Subnational Disaggregation

1. Understanding the 200-Fold Interstate Disparity

National averages conceal extreme subnational inequalities. In India, public dental surgeon availability varies by more than 200-fold across states: from 1 public dentist per 1,500 children in small Union Territories (such as DNH&DD and Lakshadweep) to 1 dentist per 300,000+ children in populous rural states (such as Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Bihar).

Tab 7: Methodology, Equations & Data Sources

Scientific Transparency

1. Purpose & Reproducibility

Tab 7 provides full mathematical transparency, documenting every formula, recursion, probability distribution, and data citation. It proves mathematically why discrete event simulation is superior to static ratio approximations.

2. Key Equations & Scientific Explanations

  • Lindley Queuing Recursion: Models patient event queues at each workstation:
    Start[j] = max(Ready[j], Free_Time[r]),   Served if Start[j] < 480 min
    Guarantees that a child cannot begin service before either they finish triage or the designated provider becomes free, and strictly enforces the 480-minute shift cut-off.
  • Multi-Month Pool Depletion & Recall Adjustment: In months 2–10 of a school year, recall visits consume camp slots:
    Rep[g] = min(ρ × S[g], U[g]),   New[g] = min(S[g] - Rep[g], P[g] - U[g])
    Ensures that only unique, previously unreached children count toward annual population coverage.
  • Duration-Adjusted YLDs (Years Lived with Disability): Incorporates multi-year biological duration with a standard 3% annual social discount rate:
    YLD_dur = ∑ Eff_New[g, i] × DW[i] × [ (1 - e^(-0.03 × dur[i])) / 0.03 ]
    Accounts for the fact that an ART/GIC restoration preserves tooth integrity for 3–5 years, providing prolonged disability prevention.
  • Dedicated Workforce Gap Capacity:
    Dedicated_Dentists_Needed = Unmet_Need / 1,889
    Based on the empirical throughput of 1 dedicated school dental surgeon conducting 16 camps/month for 10 months with team delegation, treating ~1,889 unique children per year.

3. Primary Empirical Data Sources

  • Public Dental Workforce: National Health Profile (NHP 2022, Central Bureau of Health Intelligence, Directorate General of Health Services, MoHFW, Government of India).
  • Epidemiological Burden: National Oral Health Survey and GBD 2019 Oral Disorders Collaborators.
